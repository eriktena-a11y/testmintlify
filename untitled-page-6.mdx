You are going to stand behind this plan in front of a client. Six commitments explain most of what you will see.

## One plan, many versions

Each plan lives on its own planning conversation, and every change Hazel publishes lands as the next version. Older versions stay readable, and the version control in the plan's top bar pages between them. A household can hold more than one plan, each with its own history.

A plan changes when you ask it to, so a statement that arrives next month does not quietly move the numbers in a plan you already have. **Regenerate**, in the household's **Financial Planning** tab, rereads the household's current information and publishes a refreshed version of the same plan. See [Versions and regenerating](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/versions-and-regenerating).

## Evidence over invention

When a fact is missing, Hazel either asks you for it or says in the plan that the figure is unavailable, and labels any assumption with what it rests on.

The second of the three build steps stops and waits for you, as [Getting started](/guides/financial-planning/overview/getting-started) describes. A finished plan can carry up to three short questions too, each with Hazel's best answer filled in, where it came from, and what it does to the numbers. Leave it and press **Looks right**, or change it and press **Use this**.

<Note>
  A vague plan usually means vague underlying data. Adding a statement or a recent tax return does more for plan quality than rephrasing.
</Note>

## Your firm's plans, not generic ones

Two firms with identical client data should produce recognizably different plans. Four things decide it, all in Settings, then Planning, which firm owners maintain.

- **Base template.** Its **Plan design** card holds the layout: what a plan contains and in what order. **Design layout** opens it for editing.
- **Client persona.** Each persona adjusts the layout, instructions and some numbers for one type of client. Hazel matches the household to the closest persona, records the match, and uses the base template alone when none fits.
- **Instructions.** Written guidance for content, emphasis and voice.
- **Assumptions.** The numbers every projection runs on, from the success threshold to return, volatility, inflation and tax.

How closely Hazel follows your layout is set on the same **Plan design** card, in one of three modes, and a firm that has not chosen yet starts on Flexible. See [Strictness](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/strictness).

## Math through named methods, never free-hand

Every figure a client might rely on comes from a defined calculation method with named inputs, never from arithmetic Hazel works out while writing. Select a figure with sources behind it and **Provenance and calculations** shows those sources, the method and the assumptions used. A stated fact opens on its source alone. A figure with nothing behind it is not selectable at all.

Precedence runs highest first:

- **Guardrails.** Your never-do-this rules are absolute. No instruction, persona or request crosses one, and a persona can add a guardrail but never remove one.
- **A number you set for this plan.** The **Plan settings** panel, in the top bar of a plan open in Ask Hazel, shows each planning number with the layer that set it. See [Solver assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/solver-assumptions).
- **The persona, then the firm.** Where a plan sets nothing, the matched persona's overrides apply. Where the persona sets nothing, your firm's settings do.
- **Instructions.** They never set planning inputs, so a number you want honored belongs in an assumption field, not a sentence of guidance.

Each plan's **Appendix** lists the settings it was built on in an **Applied settings** block, marked Firm, Persona or This plan.

## Levers over rebuilds

Hazel fits each plan with the dials that matter for this household: those that appear as sliders, switches and menus recompute the figures in front of you as you move them. Moving one changes what you are looking at, not the saved plan. Reordering a ranked list counts as a decision instead, so it stages a change for you to save.

When a plan does not carry the dial you want, ask Hazel in the plan's conversation to model the change and Hazel publishes it as a new version.

## The advisor is the author

Hover a line of prose and a pencil appears, in either view. There is no global editing mode, and the **Edit** button in the chat view's top bar opens your conversation with Hazel about the plan instead.

A figure inside a sentence is a chip that stays in sync with the plan, so you can rewrite the sentence around it but not retype the number. To move one, change the assumption behind it or the data underneath it.

Your edits collect in the bar at the bottom of the plan and become a version only when you press **Save**. See [Editing a plan directly](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/editing-a-plan-directly).
