**Market assumptions** are the capital-market figures behind every projection your firm produces. They decide what "the portfolio grows" means, how wide a range of outcomes is, and what a year of spending costs two decades from now.

They live in **Settings → Planning → Market assumptions**, a firm owner's screen. Like the rest of Planning settings, there is no Save button: edits save themselves.

## One set, edited in place

Your firm has one set of market assumptions from the moment the screen first opens, already filled in with Hazel's published figures, and every plan is built on that set. This screen edits it directly. There is no draft copy to build alongside it, no future-dated set to prepare, and no separate step that switches a new set on. Each change saves as you make it and is in force for the next plan anyone builds.

Along the top of the screen:

- The **name** field labels the set for whoever opens the screen next.
- A line counting how many values you have edited.
- **Reset all to source**, which appears once there is something to reset and returns every row to Hazel's figure.
- **Publish to firm**, which stays unavailable until you have changed a row. Pressing it flashes "Published" and changes nothing else, because the edit was saved when you made it. An edited row also wears a badge reading "Applies to all new plans when published". Read that as already applying, because there is no publishing step left to wait for.

<Tip>
  Name the set for the decision it holds. "2026 committee set" tells a colleague what they are looking at. "Conservative v3" does not.
</Tip>

The figures start as Hazel's own published set. A firm makes them its own by moving rows, not by choosing a different research source, so there is nothing to pick before you start.

## The four sections

| Section | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Returns** | The expected returns used to grow assets in every plan |
| **Volatility** | The variability that market simulations draw on |
| **Inflation** | The general price growth applied to spending and goals |
| **Taxes** | The effective rates used for tax-aware projections |

### What the figures mean

The return rows are nominal long-term figures, one compound annual rate per asset class. Inflation is modeled separately and applied to spending and goals, so enter the nominal returns your committee agreed. A real return entered here removes inflation twice over, and every plan comes out more pessimistic than your house view.

Volatility rows are annualized standard deviations, which is where a simulated spread of outcomes gets its width.

### How much detail to model

Each of the four sections carries its own two-segment toggle, **Simple** or **By sub-asset class**, and you choose per section.

- **Returns** and **Volatility.** Simple is three rows: Equity, Fixed income, and Cash & equivalents. By sub-asset class is nine: four equity classes, four fixed-income classes, and cash.
- **Inflation.** One row either way. Hazel holds a single general price-growth figure rather than inventing a second one, so switching this section changes nothing on screen.
- **Taxes.** Simple is one row, **Effective tax rates**, carrying a single blended figure. By sub-asset class splits it into **Ordinary income**, **Long-term capital gains** and **Qualified dividends**, grouped under a heading that names rate type rather than asset class. If you went looking for a capital-gains rate and found one blended number, that toggle is what you were missing.

A firm arrives on Simple returns, by-sub-asset-class volatility, Simple inflation and Simple taxes. The mix is deliberate, so an uneven screen is not a mistake.

<Note>
  Hazel's Simple Equity and Fixed income figures are the equal-weight average of the four sub-asset-class rows in the same section, which is why they read like a blend. Past that, the two levels are independent. Each keeps its own rows, an edit you made at one level is remembered and left out of plans while the other level is showing, and only the rows visible at the level you have selected reach a plan. Edits you cannot currently see still count in the edited-values line, so that number can run ahead of what is on screen.
</Note>

## Changing a row

Every row is a slider. Drag it, or nudge it with the arrow keys, and the reading beside it follows. That reading is a display rather than a field to type in. Returns, volatility and inflation move in tenths of a percent and tax rates in half points, so a figure between two steps cannot be set here. The [Assumptions reference](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/reference) lists every range.

An edited row says so. Its provenance line changes from the source label to **Set by You**, a badge appears, and a small **Reset to source** control sits beside it. The section header gains a **Custom** badge naming what the section is based on. Reset to source is also the only way back to the exact published figure, since the slider steps cannot always land on it.

Move rows sparingly and deliberately. Two considered changes are a firm's house view. A screen where every row has been moved is a spreadsheet nobody will be able to explain in a year.

## The Monte Carlo switch

A switch labeled **Monte Carlo** sits in the header of the Volatility card. One switch covers the whole set, whatever detail level each section is on.

On, Hazel models many market paths, which is where a range of outcomes and a probability of success come from. Off, Hazel is told not to model paths for this set, so expect plans to lean on single-path figures instead of a spread. Leave it on unless you have a specific reason not to.

## What plans do with these

A figure that carries its workings can be selected in the plan, and **Provenance and calculations** names the assumption behind it, its value, and where the value came from: Hazel's default set until your firm moves that row, your firm afterwards. A figure with no workings behind it renders as plain text and does not open, so not every number on a page is selectable.

The plan's **Appendix** gathers the whole picture under **Applied settings**: a **Market scenario** row, then the rows your firm or this plan changed, with a footnote saying the rows not listed follow the set's published values. That scenario row identifies the set rather than repeating the name you typed in settings, so treat the name as bookkeeping for your own team.

One plan can differ from the firm set. Open a plan from its chat and the **Plan settings** panel lists the market rows your firm has hand-edited, adjustable for that plan alone and committed with **Apply and replan**. A firm that has hand-edited nothing has no adjustable market rows there, which is the usual case early on. That panel belongs to the chat view; a plan opened on the household's **Financial Planning** tab has no Plan settings control, and the route to changes there is the plan's own chat.

<Warning>
  Changing a figure here changes what new plans and rebuilds are built on. A plan version you leave alone keeps the figures it was built with, which is why an older version can disagree with the screen in front of you. To bring a household onto new figures, use **Regenerate** in the top bar of the household's **Financial Planning** tab.
</Warning>

## Reviewing them

Once a year, with your investment committee. Mid-year changes are hard to explain to a client and harder to remember a quarter later.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Settle the figures before you open the screen">
    This screen edits the set every plan is built on, so there is no draft to circulate for comment. Bring agreed numbers to it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the rows, then read the counter">
    The edited-values line is your record of how far the set has moved from Hazel's figures. Check the provenance line on each row you touched, and remember that a level you are not showing can be holding edits too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebuild two plans you know">
    A household near retirement and an accumulator. Regenerate each from the household's **Financial Planning** tab, then read what moved and by how much.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Back out anything that surprises you">
    **Reset to source** on the row, or **Reset all to source** to return the whole set to Hazel's figures.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rename the set, then decide who gets regenerated">
    Name it for the date you adopted it, so anyone opening the screen later can see which decision they are reading. Existing plans stay as they were built, so regenerate the households going into a review.
  </Step>
</Steps>
