A **module** is a named piece of planning. Putting one in your layout says this plan gives that topic a treatment of its own. Leaving it out says the plan covers the topic only where it comes up.

Modules let you shape a plan by subject instead of block by block. Add **Roth conversion** and you get the year-by-year conversion schedule and the bracket work in one move, in the part of the plan where they belong.

## The two shapes

  <Card title="Modules that add a tab" icon="table-columns">
    Five of Hazel's modules are a whole tab: **Cash flow**, **Retirement**, **Insurance**, **Investments** and **Estate plan**. Adding one creates that tab inside **Getting there**, holding the blocks that belong in it. Their cards in the library wear a small **Tab** badge.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Modules that add blocks" icon="plus">
    The other fourteen add their pieces inside a section or tab. **Net worth & balance sheet** lands in **Today**, **Emergency fund** and **Debt paydown** in **Cash flow**, **Roth conversion** and **Social Security optimization** in **Retirement**.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Each module has a default home, and its card names that home before you click. Once a module has landed you can move it wherever you want it.

## Finding what you can add

Open the editor with **Design layout**, then the **Modules** tab in the left rail. Hazel's nineteen modules are listed there, grouped by planning area, with your firm's own modules underneath. This is also how you audit a design you inherited:

- Modules already in the design carry an **In plan** badge. Every group header counts them, as in "4 of 6 in plan".
- The **Not in plan** chip filters the list down to what you are missing, and **Search modules…** finds one by name.
- A module you do not have shows a line saying where it would land: "Adds the Retirement tab to Getting there", "Adds to the Cash flow tab", or "Adds to the Retirement tab (creates it)" when the tab it needs is not in your design yet.
- **Brings** lists the pieces the module carries, and **Preview** draws their shapes.

<Tip>
  You can get here in one click from **Settings → Planning**. Under the plan map on the **Plan layout** card, the module count is followed by **See what's left out**, which opens the editor on this list.
</Tip>

## Adding a module

Choose **Add to plan** on the module's card. Hazel drops the module in, selects it, and scrolls the canvas to it, then confirms in a message that names the section or tab it landed in and carries an **Undo**. That name is your check that it went where you expected.

Two things decide whether the add does what you meant:

**It arrives set to When it applies.** Hazel then includes it when the household's facts call for it, which is why a module you added may not show up in the next plan. If the topic belongs in every plan your firm writes, use the chevron beside **Add to plan** and pick the always-include option instead, or change it afterwards from the module's row in the **Outline**. On **Flexible** strictness, where a new firm starts, Always reads as a strong preference rather than a rule. See [Strictness](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/strictness).

**If the tab is already there, Hazel asks.** Adding a tab module whose tab already exists opens a dialog headed **Already in this design**, offering **Add it there** or **Create a second one**, so you cannot end up with two Retirement tabs by accident. Where an earlier add left the tab behind but empty, the button offers to furnish that tab instead of adding another one.

**Your firm's own modules follow your selection.** A custom module carries no default home, so it lands where you are looking on the canvas. One that adds blocks joins the tab or section you have selected. One that is its own section becomes a tab of the top-level section your selection sits inside. So select the destination first, then read the card's destination line, which is worked out from your live selection and names the real landing spot. With nothing selected, a blocks module falls into the plan's first section, which is rarely where you meant it. Hazel's own nineteen modules ignore your selection and go to the home their card names.

## Removing a module

Every module row in the **Outline** has a small icon at its left. Open it and choose **Remove from plan…**. For a module that is a tab, that row is the tab itself. The same action sits on the module's card in the **Modules** tab, behind the card's more-actions button. The trash can that appears on a plain section or tab row is a different control: it deletes structure, and takes everything inside it. A tab that a module owns carries no trash can at all, so that menu is its only way out.

A removal also takes the module's untouched pieces with it, along with any other module that was living inside a tab it owned. Hazel names everything that went in a message, says which edited pieces it kept, and offers an **Undo** that works until your next change. After that, the editor's undo arrow.

If you had edited the module's blocks, Hazel stops to ask first, under the heading **Save as a custom module first?**. You can **Save & remove**, which files your version in your firm's module library under a name you choose so you can add it back to any plan design later, or **Remove without saving**, or **Cancel**.

## What leaving one out means

A module that is not in your layout means **no dedicated treatment of that topic**. It does not mean the topic is banned.

Broader modules still fold narrower topics in. With **Roth conversion** absent, a retirement or tax section can still name a conversion opportunity in passing. What it will not do is produce a year-by-year conversion schedule as its own piece of the plan.

<Note>
  So the question is whether this plan needs a conversion treatment of its own. If it does, add the module. If it is context, leave it out and let the broader sections carry it.
</Note>

It works the other way too: **a narrow module you add takes its topic over from the broader ones.** Add **Retirement income sequencing** and the sequencing story moves out of the general retirement narrative into a treatment of its own, which does change how the retirement section reads.

## Modules of your own

**New module…** sits at the bottom of the Modules tab, under your firm's modules. It asks for:

- **Name** and **Description**, both of which show on the module's card in the library.
- **How it appears in the plan**: **Its own section**, which becomes a tab, or **Adds blocks to sections**, which is a single block joining a section or tab already in the plan.
- **Instructions to Hazel**: what this module should cover whenever it appears.
- **Start from**: **Let Hazel write it** leaves the tab deliberately empty and has Hazel compose it from your instructions in every plan it appears in. **Copy existing** starts from another module's blocks. **Design now** drops the new module into the design immediately so you can shape it by hand. The blocks shape offers the last two only.

**Create module** files it in your firm's library right away, so it is available on the base template and on every persona even if you never save the layout you were editing. Under **Let Hazel write it** or **Copy existing** it waits in the library until you choose **Add to plan**, the same as any other module.

Worth building when your firm has a treatment of its own: a proprietary risk framework, a specific concentration analysis, a client-facing scorecard, a section your compliance review requires.

<Tip>
  Name a custom module the way you would name it to a client, and write its description as what it produces. That naming is how you and your colleagues will find it in six months.
</Tip>

## Choosing what to enable

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with the core">
    Hazel's default design carries eight modules: the six core planning ones, plus **Retirement** and **Monte Carlo success**. Leave them in place unless a plan you write genuinely has no use for one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the specialists your clients need">
    Equity compensation if you serve technology employees. RMD calculation and retirement income sequencing if you serve retirees. Education funding if you serve families with school-age children. Charitable giving and gifting work if you serve donors.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do not turn on everything">
    Hazel ships nineteen modules, and a plan carrying all nineteen is a document nobody reads. Every module you add is another section a client has to get through to reach the decision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Differentiate by persona, not by household">
    Do not add a module to the base template because a tenth of your clients need it. Add it to the persona that covers those clients. Your first edit to a persona's design gives that persona a copy of its own, which stops tracking the base template. See [Inheritance and re-syncing](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/inheritance).
  </Step>
</Steps>
