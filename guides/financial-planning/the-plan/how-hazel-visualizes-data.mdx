You will present these visuals out loud, so each one has to carry the point you are making at that moment. Hazel picks the form that fits the claim: a **Donut chart** says "this total splits like this," a **Range of outcomes** says "we do not know the answer, we know the spread."

So the visual you get is sometimes not the one you would have named. Ask for a different one.

## The families of form

These are the product's own names: search the designer palette for them, or name them in a request.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Words">
    **Narrative** for a few sentences in plain language, written for this household; Summary, Today and Goals each open with one. **Mission statement** for the household's own words about what the money is for, shown as a quote when the household has said it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Headline numbers">
    **Key numbers** for a row of headline figures at the top of a section. **Highlight cards** for short callouts: what is working, what needs attention. **Info cards** for a card per item: one per goal, account or action item.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Probability">
    **Success gauge** for probability of success today next to probability with the plan. **Probability bar** for one probability shown against a target. **Range of outcomes** for the spread of results across Monte Carlo simulations.

    A modeled probability above your firm's display ceiling shows as greater than that ceiling, with a short note under the figure. The ceiling is ninety-nine percent unless your firm changes it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Time">
    **Scenario paths** for wealth over time on the current path next to wealth with the plan. **Stacked columns** for yearly columns split into their sources. **Inflows & outflows** for money in and money out, side by side over the years. **Whole-life cash flow** for every year's money in, out and remaining, as a chart plus a table. **Time spans** for when things start and end. **Timeline** for steps in order.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Composition">
    **Donut chart** for how a total splits across its pieces. **Breakdown chart** for one total broken into its parts. **Mix comparison** for labeled strips showing how several totals each split, the form for current mix next to target mix.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Comparison">
    **Before & after** for key figures with and without the recommendations. **Scenario comparison** for two or three choices side by side over the same rows. **Progress to target** for stacked pieces measured against a target, where the gap is the point. **Change table** for rows moving from one value to another, with a total. **Bar chart** for amounts compared across years or categories, including a plain largest-first ranking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ranking and priority">
    **Ranked list** for the Social Security claiming decision: what each start age pays a month, plus the total collected by a planning age the household picks. For any other ranking, ask for a **Bar chart**. **Priority list** for Hazel's ranked priorities for this household, with blocks able to sit inside a priority. **Savings order** for where each next dollar of savings should go, in order.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Decisions and actions">
    **Recommendation** for one clear recommendation and the reasoning behind it. **Decision log** for decisions made, approved and still open. **Questions** for what the household needs to confirm or correct. **Asset location moves** for which holdings move to which accounts, and why.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Structure and interaction">
    **Levers to try** for dials such as savings rate or retirement age, with every figure bound to one moving as you drag it. See [Levers and scenarios](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/levers-and-scenarios). **Tab group** for turning a set of blocks into tabs. **Table** for rows and columns: accounts, amounts, schedules.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

For what each form needs to work, see the [Visual reference](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/visual-reference).

## Asking for a different visual

Ask in plain English, on any part of the plan:

```text
Replace the retirement bar chart with the range of outcomes. I want
them to see the spread, not a single line.
```

```text
Turn the Roth conversion schedule into a table with a column for the
bracket it fills.
```

- **Naming the argument works better than naming the chart.** "I want them to see the spread" gets you the right form even when you name the wrong one.
- **A form Hazel cannot fill will not appear.** A **Scenario comparison** needs options whose numbers genuinely differ. When the plan's data cannot support the form you named, Hazel says so and offers the one that fits. A **Range of outcomes** reads as a spread only while the **Monte Carlo** switch is on in [Market assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/market-assumptions), where it starts on.

<Tip>
  For a visual in every plan your firm builds, put it in your layout in [Plan design](/guides/financial-planning/plan-design/overview) rather than asking plan by plan, and set how closely Hazel follows that layout with the [strictness](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/strictness) control.
</Tip>
