Every visual in a plan is making a claim. A **Donut chart** says "this total splits like this." A **Range of outcomes** says "we do not know the answer, we know the spread." A **Progress to target** says "here is the gap, and here is what closes it."

Hazel picks the form that matches the claim the plan needs to make at that point. That is why naming the argument in a request gets you further than naming the chart, and why the form you get is sometimes not the one you would have named.

## What every visual has to obey

**A visual agrees with its own numbers.** A **Before & after** is given the two figures and works out the difference itself. The parts of a **Breakdown chart** add up to the total it names. On the claiming-age ranking, the best start age is computed from the lifetime totals rather than chosen. A **Scenario comparison** can put a **Better** badge on the option a row favors, but not on a row where the options come out the same.

**Form follows the argument.** Ask for a chart that cannot carry the claim and Hazel offers the form that can.

## The families of form

These are the names the product itself uses, so you can search the designer palette for them and use them in a request.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Words">
    **Narrative** for a few sentences in plain language, written for this household. In the shipped layout, Summary, Today and Goals each open with one. **Mission statement** for the household's own words about what the money is for, shown as a quote. It appears when the household has actually said it, so plenty of plans carry none.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Headline numbers">
    **Key numbers** for a row of headline figures at the top of a section. **Highlight cards** for short callouts: what is working, what needs attention. **Info cards** for a card per item, such as one per goal, account or action item.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Probability">
    **Success gauge** for probability of success today next to probability with the plan. **Probability bar** for one probability shown against a target. **Range of outcomes** for the spread of results across Monte Carlo simulations, which shows a modeled result as a range rather than as a single line.

    A modeled probability above your firm's display ceiling is shown as greater than that ceiling rather than as one hundred percent, with a short note under the figure saying so. The ceiling is ninety-nine percent unless your firm changes it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Time">
    **Scenario paths** for wealth over time on the current path next to wealth with the plan. **Stacked columns** for yearly columns split into their sources. **Inflows & outflows** for money in and money out, side by side over the years. **Whole-life cash flow** for every year's money in, out and remaining, as a chart plus a table. **Time spans** for when things start and end. **Timeline** for steps in order: what happens first, next, later.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Composition">
    **Donut chart** for how a total splits across its pieces. **Breakdown chart** for one total broken into its parts. **Mix comparison** for labeled strips showing how several totals each split, which is the form for current mix next to target mix.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Comparison">
    **Before & after** for key figures with and without the recommendations. **Scenario comparison** for two or three choices side by side over the same rows. **Progress to target** for stacked pieces measured against a target, where the gap is the point. **Change table** for rows moving from one value to another, with a total. **Bar chart** for amounts compared across years or categories, including a plain largest-first ranking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ranking and priority">
    **Ranked list** for the Social Security claiming decision: what each start age pays a month, plus the total collected by a planning age the household picks. For a straightforward ranking of anything else, ask for a **Bar chart**. **Priority list** for Hazel's ranked priorities for this household, with blocks able to sit inside a priority so each one carries its own supporting detail. **Savings order** for where each next dollar of savings should go, in order.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Decisions and actions">
    **Recommendation** for one clear recommendation and the reasoning behind it. **Decision log** for decisions made, approved and still open. **Questions** for what the household needs to confirm or correct. **Asset location moves** for which holdings move to which accounts, and why.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Structure and interaction">
    **Levers to try** for dials such as savings rate or retirement age. Some dials recompute in your browser, and every figure bound to one moves as you drag it. A dial that needs Hazel to re-solve the plan shows no control at all, so ask for that change in chat instead. See [Levers and scenarios](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/levers-and-scenarios). **Tab group** for turning a set of blocks into tabs. **Table** for rows and columns: accounts, amounts, schedules.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

For the full list with what each form needs to work, see the [Visual reference](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/visual-reference).

## Asking for a different visual

You can ask, in plain English, on any part of the plan:

```text
Show the allocation as a mix comparison instead of a donut, so the
current and target mixes sit side by side.
```

```text
Replace the retirement bar chart with the range of outcomes. I want
them to see the spread, not a single line.
```

```text
Turn the Roth conversion schedule into a table with a column for the
bracket it fills.
```

- **Naming the argument works better than naming the chart.** "I want them to see the spread" gets you the right form even when you name the wrong one.
- **A form Hazel cannot fill will not appear.** A **Scenario comparison** needs options whose numbers genuinely differ, and a **Breakdown chart** needs parts that add up to the total it names. When the plan's data cannot support the form you named, Hazel says so and offers the one that fits. A **Range of outcomes** is a special case: the band is only meaningful while the **Monte Carlo** switch is on in [Market assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/market-assumptions), where it starts on.

<Tip>
  If you want a visual in every plan your firm builds, do not ask for it plan by plan. Put it in your layout in [Plan design](/guides/financial-planning/plan-design/overview), and decide how closely Hazel has to follow that layout with the [strictness](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/strictness) control.
</Tip>
