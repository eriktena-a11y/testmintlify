Your firm's personas live in **Settings → Planning**, in the left nav under **Client persona**. The **New persona** button below that list adds one. Planning settings are the firm owner's surface, so if you cannot see Planning in Settings, that is why.

## Before you start

Have a real client in mind. A persona written from an abstraction matches badly. One written from "the Okonkwos, and the four households like them" matches well.

It also helps to have your [base template](/guides/financial-planning/settings/base-template) settled first. A persona is defined by how it differs from the base, so a base you are still rewriting makes those differences hard to judge.

## Create it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New persona">
    In **Settings → Planning**, find **Client persona** in the left nav and select **New persona**. The dialog has four things to fill in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a starting template">
    **Starting template** is the first field, and it decides what the new persona begins with.

    - **Base template**, the default, copies your firm's Hazel instructions into the new persona and leaves its plan design following the base.
    - A **Hazel preset** copies that archetype's instructions, the handful of planning figures it sets, and its own plan design. It also fills in the name, description and age range for you to review before you create anything.
    - **Copy from** an existing persona takes everything that persona has, as an independent copy. Editing either one afterwards leaves the other alone.

    Every firm starts with Hazel's three personas already installed, and a preset only appears in this list when no persona of that name exists. So the presets group is usually empty. Delete one of the three and it reappears here as a starting template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Short and recognizable, in your firm's own language: Business Owner, Inherited Wealth, Physician Household. Up to 80 characters, with a counter as you approach it.

    Two names are refused outright, because the base template owns them: **Base** and **Base template**. A name too close to one you already have is refused as well: Pre Retiree alongside an existing Pre-Retiree gets a message saying it is too close to the existing persona. That is not a bug, so pick a genuinely distinct name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the description">
    This is what Hazel matches households against, and the field says as much underneath it. It is the most consequential thing on the screen, and the next section is about getting it right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the age range, if it helps">
    **Age min** and **Age max** are optional and take anything from 0 to 120, with the minimum no higher than the maximum. The range is a hint, not a rule: Hazel will not refuse a better match on age alone. Leave it empty when age says nothing useful, as with a business-owner persona spanning thirty years.

    Set it here if you want one. Afterwards the persona's own pane carries a name, a description, its instructions and its plan design, with no age field, so a range you skip now stays skipped. The description is where you can put "typically late fifties" in words instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Create">
    The persona saves right away and its pane opens.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Then finish it

The dialog only gets the persona created. The rest happens on its own pane.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hazel instructions">
    The ordered list of guidance Hazel follows for this client type. A persona's list is its own rather than an addition to the base template's, which is exactly why starting from the base copies your house instructions across. Edit that list down to what this client type needs. See [Instructions](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/instructions).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Plan design">
    The shape of the plan. A new persona follows the base design until you change something here, and the card says which of the two states it is in. The strictness control sits on the same card. See [Plan design](/guides/financial-planning/plan-design/overview), [Strictness](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/strictness) and [Inheritance and re-syncing](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/inheritance).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build a plan and read it">
    Pick the household you had in mind and read the finished plan end to end. Its **Appendix** carries an **Applied settings** block that names the persona the plan was built from and attributes each value to **Firm**, **Persona** or **This plan**, so you can confirm the match landed where you expected. Adjust, then read again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Edits on this pane save as you make them. There is no Save button: a quiet "Saving…" then "Saved" beside the **Plan templates** heading is your confirmation. **Delete** removes a persona immediately and cannot be undone.

## Writing a description that matches well

Hazel matches on the situation a description describes, so write a situation rather than a label.

<CardGroup cols="2">
  <Card title="Weak" icon="thumbs-down">
    "Business owners."

    Nothing to match on. Every self-employed household fits this equally well, so none of them fits it particularly.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Strong" icon="thumbs-up">
    "Owner-operator of a closely held business, five to fifteen years from an exit. The business is the largest asset and the plan turns on what the exit produces, so liquidity, concentration, and the pre-exit tax picture lead."

    Rich in signals: asset concentration, time horizon, and what the plan is about.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Where they apply, include: the stage of life or career, the largest asset, the central planning question, and what makes this client type different from your others.

<Tip>
  Write the description so a new hire at your firm would recognize which clients it means.
</Tip>

## Numbers, and where they are set

The planning figures come from two firm-wide screens in the same left nav, under **Assumptions**. [Solver assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/solver-assumptions) holds things like the success threshold, the cash reserve, fees and the default Roth conversion strategy. [Market assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/market-assumptions) holds returns, volatility, inflation and tax rates. One set of figures serves the whole practice. A persona's pane carries no number fields, so if you have been hunting for one, that is why.

A persona can still carry values of its own, and Hazel's three do:

| Persona | What it carries of its own |
| --- | --- |
| HENRY | A success threshold of 85%, under the shipped firm default of 90% |
| Pre-Retiree | A five-year cash reserve rather than three, and its own default Roth conversion strategy |
| Retiree / Decumulator | A seven-year cash reserve, the longest of the three, and its own rule for flexing retirement spending |

Copy one of those personas and the copy keeps those values. When a plan is built from a persona that sets a value, **Applied settings** attributes that row to **Persona**. That is how "why does this plan hold five years of cash when our default is three" has a visible answer.

Three figures never vary by persona: the advisory fee, the terminal heir tax rate, and the number of Monte Carlo simulations. Those are set once for the firm.

For one household rather than a whole client type, change the figure on the plan itself: select the figure, edit the assumption behind it, and recalculate. See [Editing a plan directly](/guides/financial-planning/the-plan/editing-a-plan-directly). If a client type needs a different figure on every plan, the firm-wide setting is the lever you have. Set it to what most of your book should use and handle the exceptions on the plan.

The never-do rules live on the base template and apply to every plan Hazel builds. Nothing on a persona loosens them. See [Guardrails](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/guardrails).

## Common mistakes

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A persona that does not differ from the base">
    If its instructions are your base list unchanged and its plan design still follows the base, the persona is not doing anything. Give it real differences, or delete it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A preset that quietly dropped your house instructions">
    Starting from a Hazel preset brings the preset's instructions and none of yours, because a persona's list is its own rather than an addition to the base's. Read the list right after creating and add back any house instruction you rely on.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Two personas that overlap">
    If two descriptions could describe the same household, the choice between them becomes arbitrary. Merge them, or sharpen what separates them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="An age range treated as a rule">
    Age is a hint. If a client type genuinely is defined by age, say so in the description too, where matching gives it real weight.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Instructions that try to set numbers">
    "Assume 4% real returns" in an instruction does not set the return figure. Figures belong on [Market assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/market-assumptions) and [Solver assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/solver-assumptions), and a structured figure wins over prose.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A plan design forked before the base is settled">
    Once a persona has a design of its own it stops picking up base improvements. Get the base right first, then diverge only where the shape genuinely must differ. See [Inheritance and re-syncing](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/inheritance).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
