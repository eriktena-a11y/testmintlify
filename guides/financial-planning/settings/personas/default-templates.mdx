Your firm does not start with an empty persona list. Three Client personas are already in **Settings → Planning** the first time you open it: **HENRY**, **Pre-Retiree**, and **Retiree / Decumulator**. They are ordinary firm personas from that moment. Rename them, rewrite their instructions, redesign their plans, or delete them. Your copies stay as you leave them, and nothing Hazel does later rewrites them.

They are also already working. Hazel matches households against all three from the start, so your decision is which ones to keep, not which ones to switch on.

<Note>
  When a persona below leaves something out, that means nothing in its plans is devoted to that topic on its own. Hazel still covers the topic wherever it belongs in the plan, and can still answer a question about it in chat.
</Note>

## HENRY

High earner, not rich yet. Ages 28 to 44.

Accumulation is the story: savings rate, tax-deferred capacity, and long-horizon growth, with cash-flow visibility mattering as much as portfolio design.

Its instructions tell Hazel to:

- Lead with the savings-rate target and how it moves the retirement date.
- Call out concentrated single-stock risk when restricted stock units or an employee share purchase plan appear.
- Show tax-advantaged headroom, such as 401(k), mega-backdoor, and HSA capacity, before taxable saving.
- Compound efficiently toward competing goals at once: a house, college, financial independence.
- Show what each extra dollar of savings buys.

What it changes:

- Its plan design is Hazel's default set of modules minus **Monte Carlo success**. One probability figure undersells a household funding several goals at the same time.
- It sets its own success threshold of 85%, where Hazel's firm-wide default is 90%.
- Its design ships at **Flexible** strictness, so the design is a starting point and Hazel composes each plan freely.

Keep it if you serve high-income professionals in their thirties and early forties whose plans turn on how much they can save.

## Pre-Retiree

Five to ten years out. Ages 55 to 67.

Consolidation, tax location, income sequencing, and Social Security timing take center stage.

Its instructions tell Hazel to:

- Anchor to the household's stated retirement year and frame every trade-off around it.
- Model Social Security at both 67 and 70 for each earner.
- Stress-test spending at a 4% real return.
- Surface Roth conversion capacity in the 12% and 22% brackets.
- Answer a question like "can I retire at 60?" with a defensible number.
- Build and lock the reserve ladder so rebalancing does not erode the floor.

What it changes:

- Its plan design is Hazel's default set of modules minus **Debt paydown**, which is usually resolved by this age.
- It holds a five-year cash reserve, against Hazel's default of three years.
- It sets the Roth conversion strategy to **Fill to top of 24% bracket**, with a note to fill the 22% bracket between ages 60 and 72.
- Its design ships at **Flexible** strictness.

Keep it if your clients are asking when they can retire.

## Retiree / Decumulator

In retirement or transitioning. Ages 65 and up.

Distribution ordering, required minimum distributions, healthcare inflation, and longevity risk lead the story.

Its instructions tell Hazel to:

- Fund spending from taxable accounts first, then tax-deferred, then Roth, unless the household says otherwise.
- Call out the year required minimum distributions start, for each earner.
- Show healthcare cost inflation at 5.5% separately from general inflation.
- Show a band of modeled outcomes rather than a single success figure.
- Draw down tax-efficiently without running out.
- Smooth income to avoid bracket creep and IRMAA cliffs, the income thresholds that raise Medicare premiums.

What it changes:

- Its plan design is built for the income phase rather than adapted from an accumulation plan. That design leads the tabs inside **Getting there** with Retirement income, then Cash flow, Investments, and Insurance.
- It drops the **Retirement**, **Emergency fund**, and **Debt paydown** modules, and adds **Retirement income sequencing**, **Healthcare coverage estimation**, and **Investments**.
- It holds a seven-year cash reserve, the longest of the three, against Hazel's default of three years.
- It replaces the firm's withdrawal guardrail wording with its own: Guyton-Klinger plus or minus 10%, with a 20% ceiling in goal years.
- Its design ships at **Guided** strictness rather than Flexible, so Hazel follows the design and adapts where a household's facts call for it.

Keep it if you serve retirees, and expect its plans to look structurally different from the other two.

## Deciding which ones to keep

<Steps>
  <Step title="Delete the ones you do not serve">
    An unused persona still competes to match, so a retiree persona with no retirees behind it only muddies the matching. Open the persona in **Settings → Planning** and select **Delete**. The confirmation is accurate: the persona is removed from your firm's Plan templates straight away, and it cannot be undone.

    If you want one back later, select **New persona** and pick it from **Hazel presets** in the **Starting template** list. A preset shows up there only while no persona of that name exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read one plan from each persona you keep">
    Pick a household you know well and read the finished plan. Then open the **Appendix** and find the **Applied settings** block. The Persona row tells you which persona matched, and each assumption below it is marked Firm, Persona, or This plan, so you can see exactly what the persona changed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the description first">
    The description is the main matching signal, and the field says as much: Hazel matches households to a persona using its description. Rewrite it in your firm's words, for the clients you actually have. The age range under the name is set when a persona is created, so the description is what you change to move who fits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then adjust instructions and design">
    Small, specific changes. If you find yourself rewriting a persona wholesale, your base template is probably the thing that needs fixing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  All three shipped personas carry a plan design of their own, so each reads **Customized** on its **Plan design** card and offers **Re-sync from base**. If you edit your base template and your plans do not change, this is usually why: a persona with its own design follows that design, not the base. Re-syncing discards the design the persona has now and puts it back on your base design, including later changes to it.
</Tip>
