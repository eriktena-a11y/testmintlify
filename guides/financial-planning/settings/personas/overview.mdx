You plan differently for an accumulator in their thirties, a couple three years out from retirement, and a household already drawing income. Describe each of those client types once, as a **Client persona**, and Hazel matches every household to the closest one and builds the plan accordingly.

Personas live in **Settings**, then **Planning**, under **Client persona** in the left rail. Your firm already has three, installed the first time anyone opens Planning settings. Planning settings are open to firm owners; see [Roles and permissions](/guides/settings/members/roles-and-permissions).

## What a persona holds

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What you call this archetype. Unique within your firm |
| **Description** | Who this persona is for, and what their planning centers on. Hazel matches households against this |
| **Age band** | An optional matching hint, such as 55 to 67. A hint, never a hard rule |
| **Hazel instructions** | An ordered list of guidance Hazel follows for this client type |
| **Plan design** | This persona's layout, when it differs from the base template |
| **Solver assumptions** | Planning values this client type uses instead of your firm defaults, such as **Success threshold** or **Default reserve length** |

Open a persona to edit its name, description, **Hazel instructions** and **Plan design**, or to delete it.

**The age band is set while you create the persona.** The **New persona** dialog has **Age min** and **Age max** fields, both optional, and the persona screen afterwards has no age control. The persona's row in the left rail shows the band.

**Solver assumptions are not edited on the persona screen.** The three personas your firm starts with carry their own, such as HENRY's 85% success threshold. Change a planning value for the whole firm in **Solver assumptions**, or on a single plan by editing the assumption behind the figure it drives. A persona you create with **Starting template** set to a Hazel preset, or to **Copy from** an existing persona, starts with that persona's solver assumptions.

<Note>
  Read [Solver assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/solver-assumptions) for what each value does and its default, and [Where numbers come from](/guides/financial-planning/your-data/where-numbers-come-from) for editing an assumption on one plan.
</Note>

## How the match happens

At the start of a build, Hazel picks the closest fit, weighing two signals together rather than in order:

- **The description.** What the persona says it is for, held against what Hazel knows about this household, including the goals it has stated.
- **The age band.** All else being equal, Hazel matches a 58-year-old to a persona banded 55 to 67. A better fit elsewhere still beats a match on age alone.

Both read from what Hazel already knows, so a thin profile matches poorly. See [How Hazel uses the profile](/guides/financial-planning/your-data/how-hazel-uses-the-profile).

If nothing fits well, Hazel builds on the **base template** alone and records that no persona was used. That usually means you are missing a persona for a client type you serve.

### Hazel records the match

Open the plan's **Appendix** and read the **Applied settings** block: a **Persona** row names the matched persona, or reads **None**, and each planning value under it carries the layer that set it. See [Where numbers come from](/guides/financial-planning/your-data/where-numbers-come-from).

The record is stamped when that version is published, so an older version shows what it was built with rather than today's settings.

## Overriding the match

Name the persona you want up front:

```text
Build a plan for the Okonkwos using the Business Owner persona,
not the pre-retiree one. He is 58 but the exit drives everything.
```

Or name it afterwards on a built plan, and Hazel rebuilds against it.

<Tip>
  A persona that keeps getting mismatched needs a sharper description. Rewrite it to say plainly who the persona is for.
</Tip>

## What a persona inherits

A persona is built on the base template and follows it until you change something. Its solver assumptions work value by value: the persona carries only what differs and inherits the rest from your firm settings. Its plan design follows the base design live until the first edit on its **Plan design** card, which forks a copy for that persona. See [Inheritance and re-syncing](/guides/financial-planning/settings/personas/inheritance).

Guardrails are firm-wide, and a persona can add one but never remove one. See [Guardrails](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/guardrails).
