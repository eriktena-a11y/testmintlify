You are going to put these numbers in front of a client, so you need to know where each one came from. Most figures in a plan carry their own audit trail, and you open it by selecting the figure.

## Selecting a figure

A figure with something recorded behind it carries a dotted underline. Select it and a panel slides in from the right of the plan. Hover it first and a tooltip tells you what you will get: "How this was calculated" where there is math behind the figure, "Where this figure comes from" where there is only a source.

Not every figure opens. A figure with no dotted underline is plain text, either because nothing was recorded behind it or because it sits inside another control that owns the click. Where a figure you need to check will not open, the plan's **Appendix** lists every recorded figure on one page.

Tiles in a **Key numbers** row have a second, quicker route. A small source icon sits in the corner of the tile. It opens a short version first, with the tile's label, its confidence and its source rows, and a **View calculations** link through to the full panel.

## What the panel contains

<CardGroup cols="3">
  <Card title="Sources" icon="file-lines">
    A **Source / Confidence / Value** table: one row per input, naming what it is in plain words, how confident Hazel is in it, and the value that source establishes. A document Hazel can link to becomes a link, and where the input is something the household said, the row carries the quoted words.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Methodology" icon="calculator">
    The calculation, step by step. Each step gives the formula in words, then the same formula with this household's figures in it and the result. Where a step's inputs are traced, each one names the source row it came from.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Assumptions" icon="sliders">
    The assumptions this figure rests on, numbered A1, A2 and so on. Each is written as a sentence, not a value, because most of them are statements and not numbers.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Methodology and Assumptions are collapsible sections, and each appears only where Hazel recorded something for that figure. A figure backed by a source alone opens a panel with one part. Where no source was recorded, the panel says so instead of leaving the space empty.

**View full breakdown**, at the top of the panel, leaves the single figure and opens the plan's **Appendix**, where every recorded figure sits on one page.

## The three confidence labels

Every source row carries one of three words.

**Verified.** A document established it, you entered or corrected it yourself, or it is something the household said about themselves and what they want, which Hazel treats as settled the moment it is said.

**Unverified.** Hazel captured it, but nobody has confirmed it. That is mostly money: a balance someone mentioned, an income figure in an email, a property value. Check it before you present it.

**Modeled.** Nobody stated this figure. Either the plan worked it out from figures it does have, or it fell back on a standard assumption because the household had none. The panel's Methodology and Assumptions sections tell you which. A figure that reads Unknown is a third case: nothing could source it at all. Modeled is what you want on anything about the future. On anything about today, treat it as a prompt to go find the document.

Confidence is shown on source rows, in the figure's panel and in the Appendix, and on the quick look from a tile. It is not printed beside figures in the plan's prose, so the way to check a figure is to open it.

<Note>
  Confirming a fact happens in the household's profile, not in the plan. Settle it in the [profile review queue](/guides/intelligent-client-profiles/managing-client-data/reviewing-and-verifying) and rebuild, and the source reads verified next time.
</Note>

## Changing an assumption from the panel

Where an assumption can be changed for this plan, it carries an **Edit** button. Selecting it opens the sentence for rewriting, and **Queue change** adds your version to the plan's recalculation queue.

Nothing recalculates yet. The bar at the bottom of the plan counts what is waiting, for example "2 changes needing recalculation". **Recalculate** there sends the lot to Hazel in one pass, and **Discard all** drops them.

An assumption with no **Edit** button is fixed for this plan. A button that will not open tells you why on hover: "Requires plan recalculation" where the plan cannot be recalculated from where you are reading it, and "Past plan versions are read-only" where you are looking at an earlier version.

## Auditing the whole plan

When the question is "what was this plan built on" rather than "where did this one figure come from", two surfaces answer it.

**Appendix.** The last entry in the plan's left rail, also reachable through **View full breakdown** in any figure's panel. Near the top sits **Applied settings**: the Client persona Hazel matched, the market scenario the projections drew from, then tables of **Solver assumptions**, **Planning assumptions** and **Market assumptions** under the headings **Setting / Value / Set by**. Market rows nobody changed are not listed one by one; a footnote says they follow the scenario's published values. Below that sits a card per figure, with the same sources, methodology and assumptions you would open one at a time.

**Plan settings.** The sliders icon in the plan's top bar when you open a plan from its chat. It shows this plan's effective settings under **Solver assumptions** and **Market assumptions**, each labeled with the layer that set it, and lets you change the inputs that are allowed to vary by household. Each one prints the range it accepts and refuses a value outside it. Nothing reaches the plan until you select **Apply and replan**, which recalculates it, and **Discard changes** throws your edits away. The commit is sent as a message in the chat, so the change leaves a record.

<Note>
  Plan settings and the figure panel share the same dock, so opening one closes the other. The household **Financial Planning** tab has no sliders icon at all: select **Open chat** in its top bar to reach the panel.
</Note>

## Which layer set a setting

A setting value is attributed to the layer that chose it. You read that attribution in the **Set by** column of Applied settings, and beside each field in the Plan settings panel.

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Firm** | Your firm's default, set in Settings, then Planning |
| **Persona** | The matched Client persona's override of the firm default. The label carries the persona's name |
| **This plan** | An override set on this plan, for this household only |

That attribution answers a question like "why is this plan solving toward an 85% probability of success when our firm standard is 90%." The **Set by** column names the layer, so you can tell a persona override from something an advisor set on this plan.

While you are editing in Plan settings, resetting a saved plan-level override reads **Inherited** until you apply, because which layer wins is settled by the replan.

<Note>
  A few inputs cannot vary by household, because they have to read the same across every plan your firm produces: **Advisory fee**, **Terminal heir tax rate** (the rate assumed on what heirs inherit from pre-tax accounts) and **Monte Carlo simulations** (how many market paths the model runs). They are not in the Plan settings panel at all, rather than sitting there disabled. Applied settings still lists all three, marked **Firm**, so you can always read the value the plan used. A firm owner changes them in Settings, then Planning.
</Note>

## Before you present a plan

Three of these are a read. Two can stop you: if the persona is wrong, or a figure you plan to quote rests on something unverified, fix it before the meeting instead of presenting around it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read Plan orientation">
    The info button on the plan's top bar. The brief has a fixed shape: the situation and the verdict, why the plan weighs these trade-offs, where the goals stand, then a bullet per recommendation showing the figures and the arithmetic behind it, and a closing bullet titled **Start here** naming what to resolve with the client first. Read **Start here** even if you read nothing else. The panel stamps the version the brief was written for, which can be an earlier version than the one on screen. Where a version has no brief, there is no info button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the matched persona">
    Open the **Appendix** and read the **Persona** row at the top of Applied settings. That is the Client persona Hazel matched this household to, and it sets the plan's shape and several of its assumptions. "None" means nothing fitted, so Hazel built on your base template alone. Where Hazel matched the wrong persona, do not present the plan. Open the plan's conversation and ask Hazel to rebuild against the persona you want, by name. Reading the plan in chat, **Edit** in the top bar opens that conversation. Reading it on the household **Financial Planning** tab, select **Open chat** first. There is no persona picker on the plan itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the probability of success">
    The figure on the plan's success gauge, and any other figure the plan leads with. Read its methodology and its assumptions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check one balance-sheet figure and one income figure">
    These are the figures clients contest. Open each one and confirm the source rows name something you recognize and trust, and that the confidence reads verified and not unverified. A document row names the source, not the date of the figure inside it, so where currency matters open the document yourself. A row backed by the client's own words shows where and when they were said.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look for unverified sources on anything you will quote">
    The Appendix is the fastest way to see them together, since every figure's card carries the same Confidence column. Where a figure you plan to quote rests on an unverified source, confirm it with the client and settle it in the profile, or take the claim out of what you present.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If a figure looks wrong, check what set its inputs before you conclude the math is wrong. The figure's panel tells you which sources it used and how they combine. The **Set by** column, in Applied settings or in Plan settings, tells you which layer chose the assumption behind it. Two things to rule out first: a persona override you did not expect, and a document that has been superseded since the plan was built.

  **Hazel makes mistakes. Review her answers.**
</Tip>
