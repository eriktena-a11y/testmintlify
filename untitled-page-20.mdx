This is the vocabulary you can design with. Every block below can be placed in your firm's layout in [Plan design](/guides/financial-planning/plan-design/overview), or asked for by name in chat on a single plan.

Names here are the names the product uses. In the layout editor they sit in the **Building blocks** shelf at the bottom of the **Modules** tab, collapsed until you open it. Each one shows its name and a one-line description, and you can drag it straight into a section. The same names work word for word in a chat request.

The groupings below are by what a block argues. The editor shelves the same blocks by kind instead: Narrative, Numbers & highlights, Charts, Tables, Interactive, Groups.

<Note>
  Two blocks have a fixed home. **Priority list** belongs in the **Your priorities** tab of Getting there and nowhere else. **Probability bar** cannot go in the **Goals** section. Everything else goes wherever the plan needs it.
</Note>

Once in a while Hazel builds a one-off visual for a household that is not in this list. It carries a **Generative** badge on the plan, and it is not something you can place in a layout.

## Words

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Narrative** | A few sentences in plain language, written for this household | Nothing beyond the plan's own content |
| **Mission statement** | The household's own words about what the money is for | Something the household has actually said about it |

## Headline numbers

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Key numbers** | A row of headline figures | The handful of figures that matter most, wherever you place it |
| **Highlight cards** | Short highlights: what's working, what needs attention | Short points, grouped by whether each is good news or needs attention |
| **Info cards** | A card per item: goals, accounts, opportunities, or a checklist | A list of comparable items |

## Probability

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Success gauge** | Probability of success today, next to probability with the plan | Two modeled results: where the household stands now, and where the recommendations take them |
| **Probability bar** | One probability today against where the plan lands, with an optional target marker | Two probabilities: today's and the planned one. Not available in the Goals section |
| **Range of outcomes** | The spread of results across simulated market paths | A band of results at two or more points in time. The band only means something when the **Monte Carlo** switch is on in [Market assumptions](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/market-assumptions), where it is on by default |

## Time

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scenario paths** | Wealth over time: the current path against the planned one | One to four paths, and a title that says what the lines actually cover, such as portfolio balance rather than net worth |
| **Stacked columns** | Yearly columns split into their sources | A value per source for each year, on one shared axis |
| **Inflows & outflows** | Money in and money out, side by side over the years | One flow above the zero line and one below it, each with a figure per year |
| **Whole-life cash flow** | Every year's money in, out, and remaining, as a chart plus a table | A solved year-by-year ledger, or up to three to switch between |
| **Time spans** | Two or three commitments side by side over the years, where the area is the total each one costs | Two or three items, each with a start, an end, and a recurring amount |
| **Timeline** | Steps in order across the years: what happens first, next, later | A sequence of events, each with the year or window it lands in |

## Composition

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Donut chart** | How a total splits across its pieces | One or more slices |
| **Breakdown chart** | One total, broken into its parts | A stated total plus the parts, which have to add up to it, and nothing negative |
| **Mix comparison** | Labeled strips showing how each mix splits | One mix, or several to compare, such as current against target |

## Comparison

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Before & after** | Up to five key figures, before and after the recommendations | Dials with a current and a recommended setting. Both figures are read from the plan's own model, never typed in |
| **Bar chart** | Bars comparing amounts across years or categories | One or more categories, and a value per category |
| **Scenario comparison** | Two or three choices side by side, with a verdict | Two or three options, and rows whose figures genuinely differ |
| **Progress to target** | Stacked pieces measured against a target, with the space above them measured and named | A target, the pieces measured against it, and what the space above means: a gap, room to spare, or money already accounted for |
| **Change table** | Rows shifting from one value to another, with a total | The value each row moves to, plus the value it moves from where there is one |

## Ranking and priority

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ranked list** | The Social Security claiming decision: what each start age pays a month, plus the lifetime total each one collects | Two to six start ages, the option the household has today as the reference, and a planning-age dial. For a straightforward ranking of anything else, ask for a **Bar chart** |
| **Priority list** | Hazel's ranked priorities for this household, one panel per priority | A place in the **Your priorities** tab. Leave it empty and Hazel picks the priorities; pin blocks inside it to require specific ones |
| **Savings order** | Where each next dollar of savings goes, in order | The rungs in order, each with an annual and a monthly figure |

## Decisions and actions

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | One recommendation, the reasoning behind it, and Approve or Ignore. Approving records it in the Decision log | A decision worth putting in front of the household |
| **Decision log** | Decisions made, approved, and still open | Nothing. It starts empty on a new plan and fills as decisions are approved, which is why it is worth placing from the start |
| **Questions** | Up to three assumptions for the household to confirm or correct, each pre-filled with what Hazel assumed | At least one assumption Hazel wants confirmed. The plan already stands on the assumed values, so answering is optional |
| **Asset location moves** | Which holdings move to which accounts, and why | An account to move from, an account to move to, and a reason for each move |

## Structure and interaction

| Block | What it says | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tab group** | Each block inside becomes a tab, and Hazel finalizes the grouping when it builds | The blocks you want behind tabs |
| **Levers to try** | Dials the household can move, with every figure bound to one moving as the dial moves | One or more dials the plan defines. Only the dials your browser can recompute appear as controls; anything that needs Hazel to re-solve the plan is asked for in chat |
| **Table** | Rows and columns: accounts, amounts, schedules | Column headings and at least one row |

## Choosing between them

Two habits make this list easier to use:

- **Pick by the claim, not the picture.** "They should see the spread" points at **Range of outcomes**. "This one is the biggest" points at **Bar chart**. "The gap is the story" points at **Progress to target**. "Which of these should we do" points at **Scenario comparison**.
- **Respect what a block needs.** A **Scenario comparison** with only one option cannot be built at all, because it needs two or three. A **Range of outcomes** rests on the **Monte Carlo** switch in Market assumptions, so check that it is on before you expect a band. When the plan's data cannot carry the form you named, Hazel offers the one that fits.
