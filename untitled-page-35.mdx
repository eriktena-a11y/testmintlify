**Solver assumptions** are the numbers and written policies the planning math runs on. Every success figure, outcome range, and distribution schedule in your firm's plans comes out of them.

They live in **Settings → Planning → Solver assumptions**, a firm owner's screen. There is no Save button. Edits save themselves when you move off a field, and a small indicator reads "Saving…" and then "Saved". If you own the firm, the **Planning settings** gear in the top bar of a plan you are reading in its chat jumps straight to this screen.

Three cards, in this order: **Probability & simulation**, **Fees & taxes**, and **Decumulation strategy**. Beside each field name sits a small tag reading either **Overridable per persona** or **Set Firm-wide**. That tag is the field's scope, and it is repeated in the tables below.

## Probability & simulation

| Setting | What it does | Scope tag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Success threshold** | The probability of success a plan solves toward, and the bar it is judged against. Below it, the plan is flagged | Overridable per persona |
| **Monte Carlo simulations** | How many market paths each projection models | Set Firm-wide |

Success threshold is the most consequential number on the screen. Raise it and plans turn more conservative: more saving, later retirement dates, lower sustainable spending. Lower it and the same household reads as on track sooner. Firms serving accumulators often sit below firms serving retirees.

The threshold is a bar the math must clear. It is not a limit on what a plan is allowed to display, which is a separate firm setting listed in the [Assumptions reference](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/reference).

Simulation count is the same for every plan your firm builds. More trials are more precise and slower to compute. The shipped value is 1,000.

## Fees & taxes

| Setting | What it does | Scope tag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advisory fee** | Annual fee drag applied to assets under management in every projection | Set Firm-wide |
| **Terminal heir tax rate** | The rate heirs are assumed to pay on inherited pre-tax balances, used to value the after-tax estate | Set Firm-wide |

Neither varies below the firm. A fee that changed household by household would make your plans incomparable, and a plan is not the place to model a fee schedule.

## Decumulation strategy

Four fields, three of them free text. This card is where your firm's drawdown policy gets written down in plain language, and Hazel follows what you write rather than a rule of its own. Every field here is overridable per persona.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Default reserve length">
    A slider, in years: how much retirement spending the plan carves into safe assets so a client never has to sell equities into a down market. It ships at three years, and it typically runs longer for households already drawing income than for accumulators.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reserve refill policy">
    No heading marks this one. It is the text box sitting directly under the reserve-length slider, and it says what the reserve is held in and when it gets topped back up. What ships:

    "Hold the reserve in cash, T-bills, and short-duration bonds. Refill it from equities in years the portfolio finished up."

    Rewrite it in your firm's terms. Hazel follows the refill rule as you state it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Retirement withdrawal guardrails">
    Your spending policy in retirement, written as rules that adjust spending as markets move: when spending gets trimmed, and when it can rise. What ships:

    "Use Guyton-Klinger guardrails: trim spending 10% when the withdrawal rate drifts 20% above its initial target, and raise it 10% when it falls 20% below. Skip the inflation raise in any year the portfolio finished down."

    Three suggestion chips sit under the box: **Savings rate**, **Debt-free date**, and **Simple assumptions**. Each one appends a ready-made sentence to whatever the box already holds, so if you want a chip's wording on its own, clear the box first. Otherwise you can end up with two policies stacked in one field.

    This is a spending policy, not an absolute rule. Rules Hazel must never break belong in [Guardrails](/guides/financial-planning/assumptions/guardrails).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Default Roth conversion strategy">
    Six choices, one at a time, setting the default ceiling for annual conversions. The chips read exactly:

    - **Stop under first IRMAA tier**, the first Medicare premium tier
    - **Cap at ACA-subsidy MAGI**, the income level where the marketplace subsidy phases out
    - **Drive off the heir's bracket**
    - **Fill to top of 24% bracket**, fixed at 24 percent rather than a bracket you pick
    - **Low-income years only**
    - **No default**

    The shipped choice is **Stop under first IRMAA tier**, with this elaboration in the box below it: "Convert each year up to just under the first IRMAA tier, so the client avoids a higher Medicare Part B/D premium."

    The elaboration box takes a full sentence, and Hazel honors it over the chip's usual behavior where the two would disagree. So a house rule like filling the 22 percent bracket for ages 60 to 72 goes there, alongside whichever chip is closest.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The three layers

Every solver value a plan uses comes from one of three layers.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Firm">
    Everything on this screen. The starting point for every plan your firm builds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Persona">
    The matched Client persona's own values, for the fields tagged **Overridable per persona**. A persona carries only what it changes and inherits the rest, so it does not go stale when you edit a firm number.

    The three personas Hazel installs for you already use this. **HENRY** sets the success threshold to 85 percent, under the shipped firm figure of 90. **Pre-Retiree** holds a five-year reserve and defaults to **Fill to top of 24% bracket**. **Retiree / Decumulator** holds a seven-year reserve and carries its own withdrawal wording. A new persona created as a copy of an existing one brings those values with it; one started from the **Base template** brings none and inherits everything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="This plan">
    One household's plan, changed in that plan's **Plan settings** panel and committed with **Apply and replan**. A short message lands in the chat when you apply, so the replan shows up in the transcript instead of happening silently.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Three fields never leave the firm: the advisory fee, the terminal heir tax rate, and the simulation count. Rather than show them grayed out, the per-plan panel leaves them out entirely.

## Changing one plan

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Plan settings">
    Open the plan in its chat, and the sliders icon labeled **Plan settings** in the top bar opens a panel on the right. On a household's **Financial Planning** tab there is no sliders icon, so press **Open chat** first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read where each value came from">
    Every row carries a chip: **Firm**, **Persona** followed by the persona's name, or **This plan**. **Withdrawal guardrails** appears here read-only, so you can see which layer supplied the wording without changing it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change what you need">
    Three solver fields are adjustable per plan: **Success threshold**, **Reserve length**, and **Roth conversion strategy**. Underneath them, a **Market scenario** row names the assumption set this plan draws from, and any market row your firm has hand-edited can be adjusted here too. A value outside the allowed range is refused with the range named, rather than quietly rounded into bounds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply and replan">
    Nothing changes until you press **Apply and replan**, which the panel's footer says outright. A small reset arrow on a changed row returns it to the inherited value, and **Discard changes** drops everything staged. The button stays unavailable until something is actually changed, and while Hazel is mid-turn in that chat.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  This is why a plan can run on a different threshold than your firm standard. Open the plan's **Appendix**, find the **Applied settings** block, and read the rows under **Solver assumptions**: each value is tagged **Firm**, **Persona**, or **This plan**. See [Where numbers come from](/guides/financial-planning/your-data/where-numbers-come-from).
</Note>

## Setting these well

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write down what your firm already does">
    Most firms have a house view on reserve length, spending policy, and conversion strategy. This screen is where it stops living in people's heads.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the firm defaults to your most common client">
    Not the average of all your clients. The most common one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Put real numbers in the free-text boxes">
    Write those three fields the way you would explain the policy out loud, with the figures in the sentence. The shipped defaults are a good model for the level of detail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Handle the one-off household in its own plan">
    When a single household needs a different threshold or a longer reserve, change it in that plan's **Plan settings** rather than moving the firm default for everyone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change one setting and rebuild a plan you know">
    Adjust a single value, rebuild a plan whose numbers you already have in your head, and read what moved.
  </Step>
</Steps>
